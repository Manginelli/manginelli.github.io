Systems & economy

Every system belongs to its world.

Mars Incremental: Cooling Gel

Problem
The water building needed a mechanic of its own, one players would feel the moment they unlocked it.
Decision
I made water a fluid: players choose where it goes. Since water gets spent, buildings run on Cooling Gel, inspired by PC water coolers and CPU cooling gel, which can be moved from one building to another at any time.
Result
Upgrading a building is no longer a one-time purchase. In playtests, players kept rerouting gel to wherever it mattered most.

Combat & class design

Each part pushes back on the others.

Demon Hunter: Battle Rhythm

Problem
The fantasy came from Illidan in Heroes of the Storm, whose attacks lower his cooldowns. Applied to the whole kit, it made haste the only stat that mattered.
Decision
Battle Rhythm touches only the strikes, the engine of each build. The rest of the kit keeps standard cooldowns.
Result
Strikes never return on a fully predictable timer, and haste is strong only while mana holds, so each spec values it differently.

AI & difficulty

Numbers and feel are tested, not assumed.

Hex Conquest, Hoplon

Problem
On mobile, an AI that plays well feels unfair, and one that plays badly feels pointless.
Decision
I tuned the AI to feel threatening while quietly favoring the player, and added adaptive difficulty.
Result
Even our QA didn't notice the AI was letting them win: it played like a real challenge. A shared win for design and engineering.
